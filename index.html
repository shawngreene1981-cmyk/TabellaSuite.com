The problem

A tag is a tessera: a small token that stands for something.

The Romans used a tessera as a ticket, a tally and a mark of identity, and it is also a single tile in a mosaic. That is what a tag should be: one small, reliable piece that fits into the whole picture. Today it is ink on card, and the picture gets rebuilt by hand.

I

Handwritten

Booth, item and price scrawled on paper, then read back by a clerk who has seen ten thousand of them.

II

Retyped

Every sale is typed into a spreadsheet or software. Slow lines, wrong prices, sales credited to the wrong booth.

III

Unseen

The data exists but nobody learns from it: which booths are hot, which are dead, what shoppers want.

How it works

Four steps, no retyping.

1

Photograph

The antiquer snaps a photo. Tabella drafts the title, description and category, and suggests a rarity badge.

2

Tag

Print a sheet of QR tags with booth, item number, price and badge. Stick them on.

3

Scan

At the register, one beep rings the item, the price and the booth. A shopper's loyalty card earns a punch.

4

Know

Vendors get their balance and a short report. The mall sees traffic, hot booths and slow ones, and shoppers' wants.
