Tabella Preferred Buyer Road Trip No. 1

Tennessee to the Carolinas

Four days, 12 shops and one very full car. We drove I-24 and I-40 across Tennessee, climbed into North Carolina's High Country, finished in the Charlotte suburbs, and found one more on the way home through Paducah, stopping anywhere that looked worth a hunt. Every shop here is best for someone. Here's who, and what to know before you go.

Day 1: Tennessee, Clarksville to Lebanon

1Miss Lucille's Marketplace

Clarksville, TN

More than 200 booths, all well kept and beautifully styled, with a cafe in the middle. This is a place to wander. One dealer, JW Mercantile, has booths all over the store, and spotting them became a game.

  • Best for: hours of browsing, perusing and hunting
  • Know before you go: restroom on site; no haggling; little to no cell signal inside
  • We brought home: a jadeite hen on nest, a brass bucket, a Strawberry Shortcake Caboodle, a metal wall hanger that will become a stained glass piece, and a handful of Pokémon cards
  • Tip: plan a few hours and have lunch at the cafe.
  • On Tabella coming soon: search the whole mall from your phone before you go, so the jadeite is the first stop, not the last.
See the Miss Lucille's Marketplace listing →

2Ms. Rhonda's Antiques and More

Greenbrier, TN

We found this one from the highway at I-24 exit 35, and we're glad we did. It's a newer, 20,000 sq ft building with 150-plus fun, well-decorated booths and real bargains. Out back is a covered garage-sale area.

  • Best for: pottery and shelving
  • Know before you go: restroom on site; haggling in the outdoor area only; little to no cell signal inside
  • We brought home: a Haeger boy planter and a second stamped planter, a white spice rack to refinish, palm tree bookends, and an old dresser drawer that will become a wall shelf
  • Tip: save time for the covered outdoor area; that's where the deals get made.
  • On Tabella coming soon: set a wants alert for Haeger, and you'll know when a booth like this one lists a piece.
See the Ms. Rhonda's Antiques and More listing →

3Cloud 9 Antiques & Such

Lebanon, TN

A small old house just off the corner of Main Street that opens into a maze of rooms. The clerk was as friendly as they come, and there were Lofthouse cookies out front.

  • Best for: glassware, figurines and collectibles
  • Know before you go: no public restroom; small card fee; no haggling; good signal
  • Standout booth: Mema's Attic
  • We brought home: a signed Danforth Pewter mini vase for $3, geese figurines, Japanese salt and pepper shakers, a silver pitcher, a sign and more glassware
  • Tip: check every room; the best pieces are tucked in the back.
  • On Tabella coming soon: a booth like Mema's Attic could have its own storefront, so you can follow it between visits.
See the Cloud 9 Antiques & Such listing →

4Antiques on 231

Lebanon, TN

Now on the square, this is a beautiful store with open booths that make it feel like a showroom. The dealers know their pieces, and it shows in the quality.

  • Best for: collectors hunting a specific piece, especially collectible glass, brass, lighting and mid-century
  • Know before you go: restroom on site; about a 3% card fee; no haggling; good signal
  • We brought home: a Fenton milk glass basket
  • Tip: come with a list.
  • On Tabella coming soon: a "What's this worth?" scan helps you buy with confidence at full market value.
See the Antiques on 231 listing →
Sun, Nov 15

Day 2: The High Country, Boone and Blowing Rock

5Appalachian Antique Mall

Boone, NC

Two floors of bargain booths on King Street. Pyrex greets you right inside the door, then it's china patterns and a maze of knickknacks to hunt through. The standout was booth 80: a whole table of brass candlesticks, deer and planters under two disco balls.

  • Best for: a little of everything: brass, Pyrex, china, knickknacks, even rocks
  • Know before you go: no public restroom; no lot, so park downtown and walk; no haggling; good signal
  • We brought home: nautical cast iron hooks, a brass vase and an egg plate
  • Tip: start at the Pyrex by the door, then work your way up.
  • On Tabella coming soon: printed barcode tags make checkout as quick as the browsing is fun.
See the Appalachian Antique Mall listing →

6Antiques on Howard

Boone, NC

A small, strip-style booth shop downtown, right next to everything. Selection is focused, so it's a quick, satisfying hunt.

  • Best for: vintage clothes and handmade birdhouses and bird feeders, plus a case of vintage cameras and film, local jams and baking mixes
  • Know before you go: no public restroom; no haggling; good signal
  • Tip: pair it with Appalachian Antique Mall and the shops around downtown.
  • On Tabella coming soon: small shops show up on the same road-trip map as the big malls, so they're easy to add to a walking loop.
See the Antiques on Howard listing →

7Front Porch Antique Market

Foscoe, NC

Another find from the road, on NC-105 south of Boone. A cute booth-style market with friendly staff and collector-level pieces.

  • Best for: stoneware crocks and churns, cobalt bottles, flow blue, lustreware and baskets
  • Know before you go: no public restroom; no haggling; good signal
  • Tip: come to browse the stoneware; it's a well-chosen collection.
  • On Tabella coming soon: save a piece to your wish list and get a heads-up if it's ever marked down.
See the Front Porch Antique Market listing →

8Antiques Main

Blowing Rock, NC

A big metal barn off US-321 with jam-packed stalls on two levels, a book shop, and a cheese and cracker tray waiting at the counter. We got there 24 minutes before closing and still found three things we love. We'll be back with a whole afternoon.

  • Best for: bargain hunters with time to dig
  • Know before you go: no public restroom; open 7 days; no haggling; good signal; more booths upstairs
  • We brought home: two unique egg trays, including a cobalt blue hen on nest, and a basket
  • Tip: give it hours, not minutes.
  • On Tabella coming soon: with a shop this packed, a booth map in your pocket gets you back to the piece you passed up.
See the Antiques Main listing →
Mon, Nov 16

Day 3: The Piedmont, Concord to Indian Trail

9The Depot at Gibson Mill

Concord, NC

Billed as the South's largest antique mall, with 88,000 square feet in a restored mill. The aisles go on and on through named rooms. Staff are friendly, and there's a free paper map at the door so you don't get lost.

  • Best for: everything; if it exists, it's here somewhere
  • Know before you go: restrooms and a cafe inside, plus an eatery in the next building; no haggling; good signal
  • We brought home: a green bench, a Fenton rose bowl, a deviled egg plate, a frame and a mallard decoy
  • Tip: plan a lot of time, and grab the map.
  • On Tabella coming soon: search the whole mall by item and booth number before you walk in.
See the The Depot at Gibson Mill listing →

10A Hidden Treasure

Monroe, NC

Quaint rows of stall shelves packed with bargains and a wood-beam ceiling overhead. This one was a favorite.

  • Best for: knickknacks and trinkets
  • Know before you go: restroom on site; no haggling; good signal
  • We brought home: a plate, a basket and a bag of knickknacks
  • Tip: the shelves are crowded, so look behind things.
  • On Tabella coming soon: shops like this can claim a free listing and show up on road-trip maps like this one.
See the A Hidden Treasure listing →

11Antebellum Marketplace

Indian Trail, NC

Coffee and homemade cookies at the door set the tone. Boutique-style, beautifully decorated stalls, all dressed for Christmas, with local honey and jams and shopping carts to fill. It's in the same US-74 complex as Peddler's Marketplace.

  • Best for: glass vases, costume jewelry and decor
  • Know before you go: restroom on site; 3% card fee; no haggling; good signal
  • We brought home: an oak wall shelf and a vintage holly vase
  • Tip: try the homemade cookies. You won't be disappointed.
  • On Tabella coming soon: shop the whole complex on one map, then buy online and pick up at the counter.
See the Antebellum Marketplace listing →

Day 4: Bonus stop on the way home

12Affordable Treasures

Paducah, KY

We spotted this one on Lone Oak Road heading home and had to stop. It takes some digging, and the digging pays off.

  • Best for: hunting ceramics
  • Know before you go: restroom on site; 3% card fee; no haggling; good signal
  • We brought home: stamped McCoy and Haeger pottery, a deer planter, a shoe planter, a heart basket and silver-plated creamer dishes
  • Tip: dig. The stamped pottery is in there if you look.
  • On Tabella coming soon: every booth's items in one search, so you know the pottery is there before you start digging.

The haul

What came home with us.

We bought at 10 of the 12 stops and spent about $865 in all. Pottery, brass, glass, nautical pieces and a few projects to refinish are headed for our booth.

Without planning it, we came home with five egg dishes: a deviled egg tray from Miss Lucille's, two egg trays from Antiques Main (one a cobalt blue hen on nest), an egg plate from Appalachian Antique Mall and a deviled egg plate from the Depot. That's a booth display waiting to happen.

Favorite small win: a signed Danforth Pewter mini vase for $3 at Cloud 9.

About Preferred Buyer road trips

How we do these.

We visit as regular shoppers, unannounced, and pay for everything we buy. We don't score shops. Every stop gets what it's best for, the practical facts you'd want before you go, and what caught our eye. After a trip is posted, we let each shop know it was featured, and they can claim a free listing on Tabella.
